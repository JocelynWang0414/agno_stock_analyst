### 4. Key Risks to the Thesis

**Sector-Level Risks:**

*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Changes in government policies, incentives, or mandates related to clean energy can significantly impact the growth and profitability of companies in this sector.
*   **Supply Chain Disruptions:** The clean energy sector relies on complex global supply chains for raw materials and components, which are susceptible to disruptions from geopolitical events, trade disputes, and natural disasters.
*   **Technological Obsolescence:** Rapid advancements in clean energy technologies could render existing infrastructure or solutions outdated, impacting the long-term value of current investments.
*   **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Many clean energy projects are capital-intensive and sensitive to interest rates. Rising rates can increase borrowing costs and delay project development.

**Stock-Specific Risks:**

*   **RTX:** As a company with diversified operations, RTX faces risks associated with broader economic slowdowns impacting defense and aerospace demand. Furthermore, execution risks on large, complex technological projects could pose challenges.
*   **NEE:** NEE's significant exposure to renewable energy development makes it vulnerable to project delays, permitting issues, and fluctuations in energy commodity prices. Regulatory changes specific to utility operations could also impact profitability.
*   **DUK:** Similar to NEE, DUK faces risks related to project execution, regulatory environments for utilities, and the capital intensity of its clean energy investments. Aging infrastructure and cybersecurity threats are also persistent concerns for utility operators.

---

### 5. Suggested Position Sizing

Given that all three companies share identical composite scores and exhibit similar technical characteristics (sideways trends with underlying bullish long-term indicators), a **market-weight** approach across these three names is recommended. This allows investors to participate in the clean energy theme without over-allocating to any single entity given the shared risks and current technical patterns. For portfolios significantly focused on the clean energy transition, a slight **overweight** could be considered, distributing capital equally among RTX, NEE, and DUK to capture diversified exposure to the theme's future growth.
